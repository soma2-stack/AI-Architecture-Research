## Plain-English result

The new idea is to give the history family an error-correcting control
geometry. A boundary point cannot activate just one weak donor: it must
activate many donors at once. Their separate recurrent-parameter gradient
coordinates then combine in the answer to one legal query.

The previous linear 1/sqrt(K) loss remains real. We do not remove it by
changing probes, relabeling cohorts, or assuming a better transfer matrix.
We construct a lower-dimensional nonlinear ball inside the simultaneous
donor cube that avoids its weak sparse axes. Its dimension is a fixed
positive fraction of K, so this changes the exponent attainable at short
write duration. Full donor trace matching is retained: the surviving read
is private credit, not local eligibility trace.

## What changed mathematically

1. A public Gaussian-existence/full-spark frame and continuous clipping
   give a ball whose every boundary point has >=K/20000 full donor writes.
   Injectivity survives clipping because >7K/16 rows remain unsaturated.
2. The inherited finite endpoint comparison tests the SAME private response
   row at all those donors, even with arbitrary other donor gates.
3. An upper frame bound converts the simultaneous tests into one gradient
   vector norm. It recovers the sqrt(K) that was lost when selecting only
   one coordinate.
4. A new auxiliary weighted energy estimate calibrates the comparison
   without repeatedly charging front errors.
5. Complete front, incoming-credit, tail, mask, and dense errors are charged
   in row/operator norm. No first-renewal truncation or global-time restart
   is used. The geometry and error envelopes allow m much larger than
   sqrt(n), while m/n remains small.
