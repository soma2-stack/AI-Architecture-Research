# Experiment 011 — results: does a 3,000-update budget solve four binary memory slots?

**Status: complete.** 15/15 preregistered runs (`complete`, 0 skipped, 0 errors), 3,000 updates each,
10 single-thread local CPU workers, 443.6 s total (budget 60 min). Plan: [`EXPERIMENT_011_PLAN.md`](EXPERIMENT_011_PLAN.md)
(committed in `9db24f4` before the confirmatory run). Exploratory 3-seed screen on a synthetic task — not a
language model, not a novelty claim, no bearing on `D=Omega(n),mT=o(n^(3/2))` (OPEN).

Evidence: raw per-seed JSON + logs in [`reports/experiment_011/`](reports/experiment_011/) (one `<model>_<seed>.json` each,
with loss windows, 250-update checkpoints, evals at 64/128/256/512, baselines, stream SHA-256),
[`tables.md`](reports/experiment_011/tables.md) and [`summary.json`](reports/experiment_011/summary.json), generated by
`python -m prototypes.rnn_llm_architecture.experiment_011_report prototypes/rnn_llm_architecture/reports/experiment_011`.
For each seed all models trained on a byte-identical stream (one distinct hash per seed).

## Answer

1. **Longer training did not reliably solve four-slot recall.** By the preregistered rule (≥ 2/3 seeds with
   whole-varied ≥ 90 % at delay 64) **no learned condition qualifies** (best: 1/3 seeds for `protected` and `gru32`).
   But the 800-update result of Experiment 010 was clearly an optimization plateau, not a hard wall: 2 of 12 learned
   runs solved the task (`protected` seed 43: 99.1 %; `gru32` seed 43: 100 %) and 6 of 12 left the ≈ 66 % per-slot
   plateau (≥ 70 % at a checkpoint) — all three `gru24` seeds partially, none of `protected_no_retain`. The other runs were **still on the plateau (loss ≈ 0.59) when the 3,000-update budget ended**; that is "not learned
   within the budget", not evidence of a capacity limit.
2. **The protected RNN did not outperform the GRU.** Mean whole-varied@64: protected 37.3 %, gru24 30.3 %, gru32 38.0 %.
   The +7.0-point gap over `gru24` is below the 10-point rule, and protected was higher in only 1 of 3 paired seeds
   (gru24 won seeds 17 and 29). Verdict: **inconclusive / no advantage**, dominated by seed variance (protected ranges 0–99 %).
   Removing slow retention (`protected_no_retain`) was worst: 0/3 seeds ever left the plateau.

## Length transfer (whole-varied, per run: @64 / @128 / @256 / @512)

Only the two seeds that solved the training length were checked: `protected` s43 99.1 / 100 / 98.6 / **64.2**; `gru32` s43
100 / 100 / 100 / **74.2**. Both hold to 256 tokens (4× training) and degrade at 512 (8×). Partially-learned runs
(`gru24`, `protected` s29, `gru32` s29) show roughly flat but low accuracy across delays (e.g. `gru24` 41 / 45 / 48 / 49 %).
Full per-delay numbers are in `tables.md`.

## Optimization plateaus and learning curves

- Every learned run sits at ≈ 59–67 % held-out per-slot at 250–1,000 updates (loss ≈ 0.6–0.7, a plateau).
- Plateau exits (first 250-update checkpoint with per-slot ≥ 70 %): `gru24` 2000 / 1500 / 1750; `protected` none / 1250 / 2250;
  `gru32` none / none / 1750; `protected_no_retain` none × 3. Exits occur from 1,250 to 2,250 updates, i.e. well after
  the 800 updates of Experiment 010 and after Experiment 009's two-slot plateau (500–750).
- Transitions are abrupt and seed-dependent (e.g. `gru32` s43 75.8 % at 1,750 → 100 % by 2,750; `protected` s43 78 % at
  2,750 → 99.8 % at the end, loss 0.044). Because several exits happened late (2,000–2,750), the unresolved runs might
  also exit with more updates; this experiment cannot say.
- Compute (identical for all: 3,000 updates, 10.5 M token positions): mean wall per run `protected` 257 s, `protected_no_retain` 226 s,
  `gru24` 191 s, `gru32` 182 s, reference 211 s; parameter counts in the table above.

The 800-update numbers reproduce (same plateau, same ≈ 0 % whole accuracy; the training stream is the same as Experiment
010's), confirming those were plateau readings. Extending to 3,000 updates lifted the learned means to ≈ 30–38 % whole-varied
for three of four models, entirely through a minority of seeds leaving the plateau. Differences from 010: more eval delays
(128, 512), 256 evaluation histories, a `gru32` condition run to 4 slots, and checkpoint tracking.

## Limitations and caveats

- Three seeds per condition; outcomes are bimodal (0 % or ≈ 100 %), so means are fragile and no significance claim is made.
- The 3,000-update cap was preregistered; many runs were still improving or stuck at the cap, so "unsolved" means "not learned
  within the budget". No longer or retuned runs were done (question not altered to chase results).
- Checkpoint accuracies (128 histories, delay 64, taken *before* the final update) differ from final evaluations (256 histories,
  disjoint seeds, after the last update), which is why a late jump appears between the last checkpoint and "final".
- `gru24` is only approximately parameter-matched (8,112 vs 8,016); `gru32` has 73 % more parameters.
- `delta_rule` receives the WRITE/QUERY grammar and slot address in its architecture; it is a separate reference, not a
  fair learned baseline, and its 100 % says nothing about generic RNNs.
- Hyperparameters (lr 0.002, batch 12) are unchanged from Experiment 010 and untuned; behaviour under other settings is unknown.
- The 400-update pilot (seed 17) was run via the launcher into the local results folder outside the repository and is not part
  of the evidence; it only checked timing, stream identity and plateau status.
- Executed locally (Windows 11, i7-14700K, PyTorch 2.13.0+cu130 with CUDA hidden, Python 3.11.9), not in the hosted CPU environment
  used for Experiments 009–010 (PyTorch 2.14.1+cpu); no hosted replication was run.
