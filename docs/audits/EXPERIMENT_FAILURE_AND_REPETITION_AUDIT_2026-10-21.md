# AI-Architecture-Research — Experiment Failures, Fixes, Baselines and Repetition Audit

**Date:** 2026-10-21 (America/Detroit)  
**Repository:** https://github.com/soma2-stack/AI-Architecture-Research  
**Input:** GitHub-only branch/PR inventories, committed source files, experiment result Markdown, saved JSON/CSV reports, independent-audit folders, and published GitHub Actions conclusions. This is a **retrospective failure-mode audit**, not a blanket claim of freshly rerunning the workloads.

## Executive diagnosis: why do failed experiments recur?

The evidence **does not** support the simplistic conclusion that researchers just ran the identical experiment over and over. Many iterations asked progressively better questions and corrected genuine problems. However, **four recurring structural errors** explain wasted work and inflated impressions of progress:

1. **Wrong success target / shortcut:** early toy recall rewarded the most recent bit instead of independent memory. Changing the task and evaluating `A≠B` transformed apparently good accuracy into a failure of actual two-slot storage. Similar contamination of comparisons appears when a structured reference has an oracle event parser unavailable to RNN baselines.
2. **Stopping training before the learning plateau ended:** Experiments 004–008 used 150–350 updates and interpreted low native recall as a deficit. Experiment 009 showed the protected network escaped the plateau around updates 500–750 in its tested conditions and achieved 5/5 training-length successes. Exp011–013 showed stronger initial-weight dependence, so merely running longer is **not always** sufficient.
3. **Not isolating the hypothesized mechanism:** random orthonormal banks and no-projection ablations matched or outperformed Walsh projection; fixed-gate and GRU runs sometimes stored values elsewhere. Experiment 016 showed exact mathematical equivalence to an ordinary generalized gated recurrence. A good result from a member of the architecture family is not proof that its branded special component caused it.
4. **Proof/scope substitution:** numerical checks became generalized to all widths, per-component upper bounds to full gradients, source-support envelopes to actual support, and additive input cost to `mT`. These are materially different statements. Counterexamples repeatedly forced corrections, especially PR26/29/33/37.

A fifth operational weakness is **documentation fragmentation**. `main`'s authoritative theory page is frozen at October 6 while 15 newer PRs are open, many stacked on one another. The index repeats two folder entries. Nothing in a green CI badge automatically promotes a claim from numerical test to independent theorem. This is a workflow/control problem, not just a math problem.

### What this sequence actually teaches

There **is** progress: the project moved from inaccurate, shortcut-contaminated metrics toward frozen tests, unequal-pair metrics, baselines, randomized timing, controlled seed splits, causal interventions, and exact-equivalence checks. What failed was the hypothesis that the special protected/Walsh architecture offered a reliably new or superior memory mechanism. The proper outcome is **retain the empirical learning-mechanism findings, close the novelty claim**, not restart EXP001 with more compute.

## D. Failure taxonomy: classify the error before choosing another run

**F1 — Benchmark shortcut (confirmed).** Two-slot accuracy on randomly queried/equal-valued pairs can look good if the model replies with the last written bit. Require **same-prefix paired queries** and report the `A≠B` subset; include exact last-write, constant, majority and independent-guess baselines. This was the decisive repair from EXP001→004.

**F2 — Optimization plateau / training horizon (confirmed, task-specific).** A failing 200–800-update model may escape the plateau after 1,000–3,000 updates, but many 3,000-step runs still fail. Before interpreting poor accuracy as lack of storage capacity, plot individual **per-seed** loss/accuracy vs updates and record failure to learn *within budget*, not structural impossibility. Exp009/011/012.

**F3 — Initialization instability (confirmed descriptive).** Exp012 shows outcome variance tied more to initialization than training stream for some architectures. A small three-seed headline hides 0/100% mixtures. Vary init and data independently; report a full small factorial, minimum not just mean, and number meeting threshold.

**F4 — Causal ablation not isolated (confirmed).** `retain_slow=False` changed multiple factors and left unused parameters counted. Walsh-vs-random bank and no-projection controls defeated specificity. A valid causal claim needs same trained seed/data, same live parameter/compute budget, a single manipulated mechanism, and both knockout **and rescue**. Exp013–016.

**F5 — Informationally unequal baseline (confirmed).** Delta-rule reference explicitly knows addresses and write events; ordinary GRU/LSTM must learn them. It is an **oracle upper-reference**, not a fair learned baseline. Equal width and nominal parameter count also do not guarantee equal compute, state, or live parameters. Exp001/010/013/036.

**F6 — Protocol/generator invalidity (confirmed).** AMS v2/v3/v4 failed benchmark setup; v5 suffered a type bug and then zero sanity yield; v6 C2 did not survive fingerprint constraints. Fixing a crash is useful **engineering**, but should not be presented as progress toward scientific discovery if Tier 1 still sees no candidate.

**F7 — Fast-stage selection illusion (confirmed).** v7 produced 8 C* promotions that all failed held-out Stage 3. v8 imposed 8-seed confirmation and promoted none. Always separate **screening** from **confirmation**; never spend an unseen locked test set to tune the search after seeing its results.

**F8 — Bad comparator or omitted term in a proof (confirmed).** PR29's selected vs extended scalar, PR33's `√n` growth, PR37's `S′` vs `S`, initial content, and kernel dependence each changed a theorem's truth. Error-budget scripts must include **all** terms and endpoint/capture/initial exceptions. An algebraic bound on one component never proves a full metric bound without a reduction.

**F9 — Wrong cost/metric (confirmed).** PR26's sum-of-support vs peak×horizon; PR35's exact-real credit dimension vs trainable prediction; QSR vs frozen dense tanh; R0 vs prescribed dense R; sampled vertex vs uniform boundary. A claim that swaps any of these is **out of scope**, irrespective of plotted performance.

**F10 — Unreproduced/incomplete evidence (confirmed instances).** Finite R20–R32 battery never ran; revised PR34 re-audit missing; PR35 independent review is reported but its complete source is not a PR35 file; Step3.7 not yet audited. Each needs explicit `NOT RUN`, `NOT IN REPOSITORY`, or `PENDING`, not a guess.

## E. Anti-repetition operating rules — adopt before the next experiment

These rules target the actual failure modes, not bureaucracy for its own sake.

1. **One question / one frozen acceptance criterion.** Before any compute, write `claim_to_falsify`, exact repository SHA, task, model scope, legal input/query contract, cost definition, primary metric, success/failure threshold, and the minimum useful result. If it cannot change a central decision, **do not run it**.
2. **Register every historical answer in one current ledger.** Include experiment ID, commit, branch/PR, code path, result path, tested configurations, seeds, baseline, numerical pass/fail, independent-audit verdict, superseding correction, and action `KEEP / RETIRE / OPEN / REPRODUCE`. Never clone a negative experiment into a new version without saying which earlier failure mode is being tested differently.
3. **Three separate evidence columns:** (a) code/test execution and provenance, (b) task-specific empirical advantage, (c) formal scope/proof status. A model can pass (a) and fail (b) while having no bearing on (c).
4. **Make baselines hard to beat and information-equal.** Include equal-width and near-parameter-matched GRU/LSTM, a compute/time measurement, learned ordinary gated recurrence, last-write/majority/random baselines, and an explicitly labeled oracle reference if used. Match data, update count, held-out queries and preprocessing.
5. **Require per-seed curves and counterfactual controls.** Same-prefix unequal histories, random update timing, distractor interference, optimized training horizon, fresh test sets, initialization-vs-stream matrix, mechanism knockouts and a rescue. Report number of successful seeds and worst seed, not only mean accuracy.
6. **Proof review before scale runs:** first derive the precise inequality, check exponents/signs/rounding for arbitrarily large sizes, adversarial zero/negative baths, nonzero initial data and captures, then verify exact arithmetic certificates. Test `n` just beyond advertised thresholds and astronomically large adversarial values. Re-running `n=512→1024→2048` cannot certify an all-n theorem.
7. **No selective-to-full or R0-to-dense promotion by analogy.** Record the actual matrix, rank-one perturbation, exact support after compensation, gradient group, selected/full readout direction, admissible legal query, and whether the bound transfers in the required **direction**.
8. **Independent audit checks the pinned revision.** After a correction, the previous audit does not automatically verify the repair. For PR35, commit/preserve the full claimed independent review if permissible; for PR37 Step3.7, obtain a fresh hostile audit of §§R3/11. Use findings against one another, not self-certifying reruns.
9. **Do not re-open retired programs without a genuinely new hypothesis.** Exp016 closes novelty of the existing protected cell; AMS v8 closes its tested grammar; PR26 closes the cheap-additive-cost path *for the original peak×horizon target*. More runs of these unchanged mechanisms are low priority.
10. **Consolidate one canonical status after review, without rewriting history.** Keep original negative results and raw outputs immutable. Add links/withdrawal notices and pin revised evidence. The older Oct6 `main` summary must no longer be used as if it summarizes PR23–37.
